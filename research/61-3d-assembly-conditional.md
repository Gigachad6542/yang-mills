# The 3D assembly: H3D-1 from single-step bounds, written out

Status:

- **Proved here.**
  - Lemma G, a summable-coupling bootstrap for two RG flows in 3D, with
    physical-coupling bookkeeping.
  - Theorem 1, which is conditional: hypotheses (H1)-(H5) imply H3D-1 of
    note 18 with η_N <= C (N + 1) λ^N, where λ = max(θ', L^{-1}). With H3D-0
    (note 53), note 18 Theorem 1 then gives P4-3D and M1-3D convergence.
- **Effect.** This replaces "outlined" in note 52 §3 by a written-out
  conditional theorem. Its hypotheses are each a single-step statement or a
  fixed-terminal-lattice statement. Their source status is listed in §4.
  The rate λ = max(θ', 1/L) explains the σ = 1 component seen in the Monte
  Carlo (notes 55, 59): the marginal part of the one-step defect does not
  contract, but in 3D it is O(ε_N) in physical units.

## 1. Setting

- Depth N. The steps j = 0, 1, ..., N go from the depth-N lattice
  (scale k = N) to the terminal lattice (k = 0), so step j acts at scale
  k = N - j.
- Dimensionless couplings: g_k² = g² ε_k with ε_k = ε_0 L^{-k}.
- Two flows carry Balaban-type effective data at each scale (CMP 102). The
  first is the depth-N flow for the measure μ_{N,s}. The second is the same
  with s replaced by s' (note 18 §2: interpolation by e^{sD_N}).
- At step j, with scale k = N - j, compare the two flows by:
  - δ_j: the distance of their irrelevant data, in the mixed norm of
    note 51 (form norm on quadratic parts, ε_0^{-1} times sup norm on
    higher orders), with large-field terms included as in (H4);
  - Δ_j: the difference of their **physical** inverse couplings
    y = ε_k g_k^{-2} = g^{-2}, as generated by the steps so far. In 3D the
    bare y is the same for both flows. Pure transport (re-expressing data at
    the coarser scale) leaves y unchanged.

## 2. Lemma G (summable-coupling bootstrap)

Suppose that for 0 <= j < N, with k = N - j,

    (i)  δ_{j+1} <= θ' δ_j + β ε_k Δ_j,
    (ii) Δ_{j+1} <= Δ_j + γ ε_k δ_j,

with 0 <= θ' < 1 < L, β, γ >= 0, and 4βγ ε_0²/L <= ½. Put λ = max(θ', L^{-1}).
Then

    δ_N <= θ'^N δ_0 + (4βε_0/L)(Δ_0 + γ ε_0 (N + 1) λ^N δ_0),
    Δ_N <= 2(Δ_0 + γ ε_0 (N + 1) λ^N δ_0).

*Proof.* Let M = max_{j<=N} Δ_j.

1. *Iterate (i).* δ_j <= θ'^j δ_0 + β M S_j, where
   S_j = Σ_{i<j} θ'^{j-1-i} ε_{N-i}. Since ε_{N-i} = ε_{N-j} L^{-(j-i)},
   we get S_j <= ε_{N-j} Σ_{m>=1} θ'^{m-1} L^{-m} <= 2ε_{N-j}/L.
2. *Iterate (ii).* Δ_j <= Δ_0 + γ Σ_{i<j} ε_{N-i} δ_i. Substitute step 1:
   - Σ_i ε_{N-i} θ'^i <= ε_0 Σ_{i<=N} L^{-(N-i)} θ'^i <= ε_0 (N + 1) λ^N;
   - Σ_i ε_{N-i} β M · 2ε_{N-i}/L <= (2βε_0²/L) M Σ_m L^{-2m} <= (4βε_0²/L) M.

   Taking the max over j,

       M <= Δ_0 + γε_0 (N + 1) λ^N δ_0 + (4βγε_0²/L) M,

   so M <= 2(Δ_0 + γε_0 (N + 1) λ^N δ_0).
3. *Insert.* Put M into step 1 at j = N, with S_N <= 2ε_0/L. ∎

## 3. Theorem 1 (3D assembly, conditional)

**Hypotheses.** For the depth-N flows, uniformly in N and s, s' ∈ [0, 1]:

- **(H1) Transport.** Re-expressing irrelevant data at the next scale
  contracts the mixed norm by θ_T := C_ab/L + C_1(L) ω(ε_0) < 1.
  Here C_ab is the sharp abelian constant (notes 44-45), which holds exactly
  at flat backgrounds (note 54), and ω(ε_0) <= d n_0 √ε_0 (note 57).
- **(H2) Generation.** The newly generated irrelevant data at step j depend
  on:
  - the input irrelevant data with Lipschitz constant C g_k² p(g_k)⁴
    (note 42);
  - the input physical coupling with Lipschitz constant β ε_k^{1-η}, for any
    η > 0. A change Δy is the relative change Δx_k/x_k = Δy/y = g²Δy of
    x_k = g_k^{-2}. Generation is O(g_k² p(g_k)⁴) times that relative change,
    so its Lipschitz constant is C g⁴ ε_k p(g_k)⁴ <= β ε_k^{1-η}, because
    p(g_k)⁴ grows only like a power of log(1/ε_k).
    (The fourth power is because CMP 102's fluctuation cutoff is |A| < p², so
    the deformation domain is |s| g p² <= α'; see note 42 §4, T63.)

  So (i) holds with θ' := θ_T + C sup_k g_k² p(g_k)⁴ < 1.
- **(H3) 3D marginal structure.** The marginal (F²) coefficient generated
  at step j depends on the input irrelevant data with Lipschitz constant
  γ ε_k in physical units, and pure transport preserves y. So (ii) holds.
  This is the superrenormalizable structure of CMP 102: there is no
  coupling renormalization, and the generated F²-terms at scale k are
  O(g_k²) in units of x_k, that is O(ε_k) in units of y.
- **(H4) Large fields.** (i)-(ii) hold for the full data, including
  CMP 102's large-field terms, with θ' replaced by θ' + C ε_k^q for some
  q > 0. This uses the CMP 102 large-field factors, which are arbitrary
  powers of ε.
- **(H5) Terminal conversion and input size.**
  - On the fixed terminal lattice, the oscillation of the terminal tangent
    defect m_{N,s} (note 18 Prop. 2) is <= C_T (δ_N + Δ_N) for tangent
    data (the derivative in s of the difference of the two flows).
  - The one-step defect D_N has initial tangent sizes δ_0 <= C_0 and
    Δ_0 <= C_0 ε_N.

**Theorem 1.** Under (H1)-(H5),

    η_N := sup_{s ∈ [0,1]} osc m_{N,s} <= C (N + 1) λ^N,   λ = max(θ' + C sup_k ε_k^q, L^{-1}) < 1.

So H3D-1 of note 18 holds with any σ < min(-log_L θ'', 1), where
θ'' = θ' + C sup_k ε_k^q. With H3D-0 (note 53), note 18 Theorem 1 gives
P4-3D: the terminal laws ν_N converge in total variation, and the M1-3D
generating functions converge.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma G to the s-derivative of the difference of the two
flows. All the maps involved are differentiable along the interpolation,
because the data stay in Balaban's analytic domain for s ∈ [0, 1] by
(H5)'s input size. [Superseded (audit 2026-10-09, T82): this differentiability along μ_s is not automatic; it is the input (L_s) of §6b, reduced there and in §6e.] (H1), (H2) and (H4) give (i). (H3) and (H4) give (ii).
Lemma G with Δ_0 <= C_0 ε_N = C_0 ε_0 L^{-N} <= C_0 ε_0 λ^N gives
δ_N + Δ_N <= C (N + 1) λ^N. (H5) converts this into the bound on η_N. ∎

**Remarks.**

- *ε_k^{1-η} in place of ε_k.* Lemma G holds with ε_k replaced by
  ε_k^{1-η} throughout: replace L by L^{1-η} in its proof. The rate becomes
  λ = max(θ'', L^{-(1-η)}).
- *Coarse steps.* (H1)-(H2) need g_k² p(g_k)⁴ small, so that θ' < 1. That
  holds for k >= k_0(g²ε_0). The last k_0 steps, near the terminal scale,
  are a fixed finite number of steps on fixed lattices. Each is Lipschitz
  in the mixed norm with some constant C_k, by Balaban's single-step
  bounds. They multiply the bound by Π_{k<k_0} C_k, which does not depend
  on N. So Theorem 1 holds for every fixed g²ε_0, with θ' and λ computed
  over k >= k_0.

**Proposition 2 ((H3) from (A_s)).** Assume (A_s) for the CMP 102 step.
Then (H3) holds with γ = C g² p(g_k)⁴, up to the replacement ε_k -> ε_k^{1-η}
of the Remarks.

*Proof.*

1. *Transport creates no marginal part.* Irrelevant data have zero marginal
   charge in each block: the block average c̄ of note 51 Lemma 4 vanishes.
   Pure transport acts on the marginal sector by the exact block average,
   which preserves zero sums (note 33 §2, Lemma 2 and its "Use"). So the
   transported data again have c̄ = 0, and y is unchanged.
2. *Generated part.* By note 42 Theorem 3 (under (A_s)), the derivative of
   the step output with respect to the input data is transport plus R,
   with ||R|| <= 2M (g_k p(g_k)/α')² in the input-to-output norm.
3. *Extraction.* The marginal coefficient is c̄, the block average of the
   F²-coefficient field c of the quadratic part (note 51 Lemma 4). It is a
   linear functional of the output, bounded by the form norm. So the
   generated marginal shift satisfies
   |Δx_k| <= C (g_k p(g_k))² δ_j in units of x_k = g_k^{-2}.
4. *Units.* Since y = ε_k x_k, we get |Δy| <= C ε_k g_k² p(g_k)⁴ δ_j
   <= C g² ε_k^{1-η} δ_j, because g_k² = g²ε_k <= g²ε_0. ∎

So (H3) is not an independent input: it follows from the same (A_s) as
(H2).

**Proposition 3 ((H5), terminal conversion).** Assume the terminal
effective density has the small-field representation
ρ_0(W) = exp(-y ε_0^{-1} A_0(W) + Σ_X E_0(X, W)) (CMP 102 at k = 0), plus the
large-field terms of (H4). Assume E_0 has Balaban's exponential polymer
decay. Then the terminal tangent defect satisfies

    osc m_{N,s} <= C_T (δ_N + Δ_N) + (large-field part of (H4)),

with C_T depending only on n_0, g²ε_0 and the terminal small-field radius.

*Proof.*

1. *Tangent as a log-density derivative.* By note 18 Prop. 2,
   m_{N,s}(W) = ∂_s log(dν_s/dW) + const, where ν_s is the terminal law of
   μ_{N,s}. So m_{N,s} is the s-derivative of the terminal effective action,
   up to constants, and osc removes constants.
2. *Marginal part.* ∂_s y · ε_0^{-1} A_0(W) has oscillation
   <= ε_0^{-1}|∂_s y| · osc A_0 <= C n_0³ Δ_N, since A_0 is a sum of 3n_0³
   bounded plaquette terms.
3. *Quadratic irrelevant part.* By note 46, |Q(F(W))| <= ||Q||_S S(Φ(W)). On
   the terminal small-field set S(Φ) <= C n_0³, because the terminal lattice
   is fixed and the curvatures are bounded.
4. *Higher-order part.* Sup norms on the small-field set. Polymer sums over
   the finite terminal lattice converge by exponential decay and contribute
   a factor <= C n_0³.
5. Summing 2-4 gives C_T (δ_N + Δ_N). ∎

**Input sizes.** δ_0 <= C_0 and Δ_0 <= C_0 ε_N follow from CMP 102's first
step at depth N:

- the irrelevant output obeys CMP 102 (25)/(33);
- the generated F²-coefficient is O(1) in units of x_N = g_N^{-2}, hence
  O(ε_N) in units of y.

So (H5) is proved, given the representations above (CMP 102 for small
fields, (H4) for large fields).

## 5. Comparison with the numerics

Theorem 1 predicts η_N ~ (N + 1) max(θ', L^{-1})^N. In the Monte Carlo of
notes 55 and 59, the leading decay is L^{-N} (σ = 1, from Δ_0 = O(ε_N) and
the O(ε_k)-weighted marginal generation), with a subleading L^{-2N} part.
This agrees with θ' < 1/L, which is consistent with the dimension-6
transport rate. The fitted two-term form X_∞ + aL^{-N} + bL^{-2N}
(note 55) is exactly what Lemma G's structure predicts.

## 6. Large fields in 3D (H4)

**First reading (superseded).** CMP 102 controls large fields only through
the stability inequality (41)/(5): upper-bound factors exp(-p²(g_j)/4) per
large-field plaquette, plus a lower bound, with extensive constants
O(1)|T|. Localized statements cannot be read off from it.

**Correction (CMP 119, introduction, printed pp. 244-245).** Balaban's
large-field papers do not stop at bounds: "now we are interested not in
bounds, but in a precise description of the effective densities in the form
of convergent expansions". On dimension he writes: "For d < 4 we have
g_0 = g ε^{(4-d)/2}, and the bound above can be estimated by an arbitrarily
large power of ε. This is enough to control expressions arising in the large
field regions surrounding the plaquette p for all steps of the procedure,
i.e., until we reach the unit lattice." The R-operation is introduced only
because in d = 4 this fails. So in 3D, CMP 119's inductive description
(Sect. 2) gives exact convergent expansions with large-field regions carried
along unrenormalized, and each carries a factor ε_j^q with q arbitrary. The
same holds for CMP 122 I, introduction.

What then gives (H4):

- **(H4a) Localized large-field probability.** Apply note 49's rooted KP
  lemma (Prop. 2 with t = 0) to this exact expansion. A region at scale j
  near x has probability <= C ε_j^q.
- **(H4b) Influence.** The tangent of a large-field activity is bounded by
  its sup norm times the insertion size, by Cauchy in s on the analyticity
  domain. So it is ε_j^q-small per region, and summing over the
  (n_0 L^j)³ blocks costs only ε_j^{q-3}.

**Status.** (H4) is a reading of CMP 119 Sect. 2 at d = 3, which Balaban
asserts in the introduction but writes out only for d = 4, plus note 49.
It is not a new construction. The line-by-line check for d = 3 is open.

### 6b. Localized s-analyticity and (H4b) (T66)

**Question.** The proof of Theorem 1 differentiates the two flows along
μ_s ∝ e^{sD_N}μ_0 (note 18 §2). Note 18 Prop. 3 gives a zero-free strip for
the normalization Ξ(s) of width π/osc D_N, which is O(ε_N^3) because D_N is
extensive (note 18 §8, F2). So Cauchy estimates in s on the *measure* cannot
be uniform in N. T66 asks for a localized replacement: D_N is a sum of local
terms with Balaban decay, so that the activities of the s-deformed expansion
are analytic on a disc |s| <= s_0 with s_0 independent of N.

**Result.** What is needed is a statement about *activities*, not about Ξ(s).
It is proved here (Lemmas 6b.1, 6b.2) from three explicitly labelled inputs:
(I-L) locality of D_N, (I-S) an analytic Lipschitz step map on a complex
neighbourhood of the real s-orbit, and (I-LF) a large-field comparison. The
unconditional claim **fails** at two terms of D_N, which CMP 102 does not
localize: the sixth-order remainder T4 and the large-field part T5 (§6b.4).
The classical term T1 is local only through CMP 102b (190), whose proof Balaban
omits. So the status is **conditional reduction**, not a theorem. The deliverable
is therefore a proof of the reduction plus a failed attempt at the unconditional
statement, with the failing terms named.

#### 6b.1 Anatomy of D_N (proved here: bookkeeping from CMP 102)

Let V be the coarse configuration on Λ_N. The first step of depth N+1 is T
applied to the Wilson density, so by CMP 102 (36), (33)-(34), (62)-(63), on the
small-field set (|V(∂p) - 1| < ε_1 on every plaquette),

    D_N(V) = c_N + T1 + T2 + T3 + T4.

- **c_N**: vacuum constants (CMP 102 (62), (35)). Constant in V; drops out of
  m_s and of every oscillation.
- **T1 (classical).** T1(V) = β_N A_N(V) - β_{N+1} A^η(U_1(V)), where U_1(V) is
  the minimal fine configuration with block average V (CMP 102b Thm. 1, p. 279).
  The second part is the leading term g_1^{-2}A^η(U_1(V)) of CMP 102 (36), (41).
- **T2 (interaction polymers).** Σ_Y 𝒫_1(Y, U_1(V)), CMP 102 (33)-(34): at least
  second order in the loop variable B, gauge invariant (26), depending on U_1
  only through X^5 (the localization property after (26)), analytic in U_1, with
  no linear term (32), and bounded by (25) with 𝔏(X) the tree length and κ
  arbitrarily large once M_1 is large.
- **T3 (determinants).** -Σ_c log det S(U_1, b_0(c)) and the contour-integral
  term (63) of CMP 102, expanded by the random-walk representation (3.185) of
  CMP 99 into gauge-invariant localized terms ("with proper exponential decay").
- **T4 (sixth-order remainder).** R_6(V), the difference between the Gaussian
  integral and its sixth-order cumulant expansion, CMP 102 (22)-(24). CMP 102
  bounds it only in sup norm: |R_6| <= O(g_0^7 p^{18}(g_0))|Ω_1|
  <= O(ε^{3+κ_0})|T^{(1)}|. It is **not** written as a sum of localized terms.
- **T5 (large fields).** If some plaquette of V is large there is no such
  decomposition. CMP 102 gives only the bounds (5), (41): an upper bound with a
  factor exp(-p²/4) per large plaquette (from (70)-(71)), and a lower bound (47)
  valid **only on the small-field set χ_k**.

#### 6b.2 The three inputs

Let 𝔅 be the Banach space of polymer activity systems E = (E(X)), X a connected
union of big blocks of Λ_N, with E(X) gauge invariant, depending on V|_{X^5},

#### 6c.1 Setting: the quadratic skeleton

Work in the dimensionless units of note 18 §8 (3D, g²ε_0 = 1, scale covariance).
At every level the Wilson flux form is S(B) = |B|² on the closed fluxes
B ∈ ran d (a factor ½ is dropped throughout). Forms are Hermitian, gauge
invariant, and Fourier-diagonal in the terminal momentum K up to aliasing.

One block step is the Schur complement. For a fine form Π and a coarse flux Φ,

    (ΣΠ)(Φ) := min { Π(B) : B ∈ ran d_fine, w B = Φ },

where w is the kernel of note 18 §8(a): w_j = b(x_j) ∏_{i≠j} (b*s)(x_i), with
b = L^{-1}1_{[0,L)}, s = 1_{[0,L)} on the fine lattice. In Fourier variables
this is the formula of `t7_gauss3d.py`, covariance side
(GF)(K) = L^{-4} Σ_m conj(W) F(p_m) W, p_m = (K + 2πm)/L, and ΣΠ = (G Π^+)^+.
`t76_convexity.py` reproduces `terminal_cov(K, L^{N+1})` from the flow to 6e-15
(output `.out`, "consistency").

*Orbit.* At level N the s-data are the log-linear interpolation

    Π_N^{(s)} = (1-s) S + s Π_1 = S + sΔ,   Π_1 := Σ(S),  Δ := Π_1 − S,

because −log ρ_s = (1−s)(−log ρ_0) + s(−log Tρ_1) and the Hessian of −log Tρ_1
at flat fields is the Schur complement of the finer Wilson form (the quadratic
part of T1; T2-T5 are not in this sector). At level N−j (j steps down)

    Π_j^{(s)} := Σ^j Π_N^{(s)},   W_n := Σ^n S   (the Wilson orbit).

Then Π_j^{(0)} = W_j and Π_j^{(1)} = W_{j+1} (the depth-(N+1) Wilson flow), and
W_n does not depend on N. A composite of Schur complements is the Schur
complement of the composite constraint, so

    Π_j^{(s)}(Φ) = min { S(B) + sΔ(B) : B ∈ ran d_N, w_{N→N−j} B = Φ }.   (6c.1)

**Lemma 6c.1 (proved).** Σ is (a) monotone: Π ≤ Π' ⇒ ΣΠ ≤ ΣΠ'; (b) positively
homogeneous; (c) concave: Σ((1−s)Π + sΠ') ≥ (1−s)ΣΠ + sΣΠ'. Hence Σ^j has the
same three properties. *Proof.* (a) a minimum of a larger function; (c) the minimum
of a sum is at least the sum of the minima; monotone concave maps compose to a
monotone concave map. ∎

**Lemma 6c.2 (proved; (R): the Wilson step is non-decreasing).** Σ(S) ≥ S, i.e.
Δ ≥ 0, i.e. r(K) ≥ 1 in the notation of §6b.5.

*Proof.* In coarse units the fine Wilson form is L|B|² (β_{N+1} = Lβ_N), so the
claim is |wB|² ≤ L|B|² for every fine flux field B. Fix an orientation j and a
coarse plaquette Y. Since w ≥ 0 has mass L² (b has mass 1, b*s has mass L),
Cauchy-Schwarz gives (wB)_Y² ≤ L² Σ_x w(x−Y)B(x)². Sum over Y ∈ (LZ)³: the
periodization of w over (LZ)³ is the constant Σ_Y w(x−Y) = 1/L, because b tiles
with weight 1/L along j (Σ_{y∈LZ} b(t−y) = 1/L), and
Σ_{y∈LZ}(b*s)(t−y) = mass(b) = 1 in the perpendicular directions (s is the
indicator of a fundamental domain of LZ). Hence
Σ_Y (wB)_Y² ≤ L² (1/L) Σ_x B(x)² = L|B|². Sum over j. ∎

(The kernel facts are checked for L = 2,...,5 by `t76_kernel_bounds.py`, part (1).
Equality is approached as K → 0, where r → 1: the sampled continuum minimum of r
is 1.000026 for L = 2, part (2).) For SU(2) at a flat background the same proof
works if the linearized covariant average is w with each term transported by an
isometry R_{x,Y}, because then |(wB)_Y| ≤ Σ w|B| still holds; that is an input
from note 54 §2 (the neutral/charged sector decomposition), not re-derived here.

#### 6c.2 Theorem 6c.3 (the orbit lies in the form class; proved, abelian skeleton)

For every L ≥ 2, N ≥ 0, 0 ≤ j ≤ N, 0 ≤ s ≤ s' ≤ 1 (order = Loewner order of forms
on ran d at level N−j):

1. **Sandwich, no loss of constants.**

       S ≤ W_j ≤ Π_j^{(s)} ≤ Π_j^{(s')} ≤ W_{j+1}.

   So the coercivity constant is 1 for every s, j, N (the value at both endpoints),
   and the upper constant is that of the endpoint W_{j+1}, which does not depend on
   N. In particular ‖Π_j^{(s)} − W_j‖_S ≤ ‖W_{j+1} − W_j‖_S, with the form norm
   ‖Q‖_S := sup|Q(Φ)|/S(Φ) of note 46 (reference S; the reference W_1 of note 46 is
   equivalent, ‖·‖_{W_1} ≤ ‖·‖_S ≤ r_max ‖·‖_{W_1}, by S ≤ W_1 ≤ r_max S).
2. **Concavity and tangent domination.** For each Φ, s ↦ Π_j^{(s)}(Φ) is concave,
   real-analytic, and with B_s(Φ) the minimizer in (6c.1),

       ∂_s Π_j^{(s)}(Φ) = Δ(B_s(Φ)),   0 ≤ ∂_sΠ_j^{(s)} ≤ ∂_sΠ_j^{(0)} = Δ(B_0(Φ)) =: (T_jΔ)(Φ),

   where T_jΔ is the transport of the initial defect Δ along the **Wilson** minimizers
   (the linearized flow of note 18 Prop. 2(v) at s = 0). Also
   Π_j^{(s)} ≤ W_j + s T_jΔ and Π_j^{(s)} ≥ (1−s)W_j + sW_{j+1}.
3. **Consequence for the tangent.** For every s ∈ [0,1],
   ‖∂_sΠ_j^{(s)}‖_S ≤ ‖T_jΔ‖_S, and the same for the oscillation of the quadratic
   part of m_{N,s} on the terminal lattice (j = N). So the s-integral of Theorem 1
   (H3D-1, quadratic sector) is controlled by the s = 0 linearized Wilson flow
   alone; the difference inequalities (i)-(ii) of Lemma G are not needed at interior s.

*Proof.* (1) Π_N^{(s)} = S + sΔ with Δ ≥ 0 gives S ≤ Π_N^{(s)} ≤ Π_N^{(s')} ≤ Π_N^{(1)} = Σ(S).
Apply the monotone Σ^j (Lemma 6c.1(a)); Σ^jS = W_j, Σ^{j+1}S = W_{j+1}.
(2) By (6c.1), Π_j^{(s)}(Φ) is a minimum of functions affine in s, hence concave. The
constrained minimizer is unique (S is strictly convex on the fibre), so the envelope
theorem gives the derivative formula, which is ≥ 0 because Δ ≥ 0. The derivative of
a concave C¹ function is non-increasing, so it is ≤ its value at 0. The two bounds
are the tangent line at 0 (test B_0 in the minimum) and the chord (concavity).
(3) A sandwich of forms 0 ≤ A ≤ B implies ‖A‖_S ≤ ‖B‖_S. ∎

need an interval argument or a Fourier estimate that is not given here.

*What this resolves of §6b.4 item 4.* The O(1) size of Δ does not matter. The step

#### 6c.3 Proposition 6c.4 (complex s: an N- and j-independent analytic disc; proved, abelian skeleton)

Let ρ := ‖Δ‖_S = max eig Δ (= r_max − 1: 3.0000 for L = 2, 5.2758 for L = 3,
`t76_convexity.out`). Let s = σ + iς with σ ∈ [0,1], and put

    ε := |ς| ρ / (1 + σρ)  (≤ |ς|ρ).

If ε < 1, then Π_j^{(s)} (the holomorphic continuation of (6c.1): the critical value of
the form S + sΔ on the fibre) is well defined for every j, N and satisfies, for all Φ,

    | Π_j^{(s)}(Φ) − Π_j^{(σ)}(Φ) | ≤ (ε/(1−ε)) Π_j^{(σ)}(Φ) ≤ (ε/(1−ε)) W_{j+1}(Φ).

In particular Π_j^{(s)} is analytic in s on |Im s| < 1/ρ around [0,1], uniformly in
N and j. The radius 1/ρ is the zero-free radius of §6b.5 (0.333 for L = 2,
0.190 for L = 3).

*Proof.* Whiten the composite fibre problem by the real form Π_N^{(σ)} ≥ S. Write
the fibre as b + Z, where Z is the kernel of the composite constraint and b is the
Π_N^{(σ)}-minimal element (b ⊥ Z, |b|² = Π_j^{(σ)}(Φ)). The perturbation Q = iςΔ has
whitened operator norm |ς| max eig(Δ(Π_N^{(σ)})^{-1}). The eigenvalues of
Δ(S + σΔ)^{-1} are x/(1+σx) with x ∈ spec(S^{-1/2}ΔS^{-1/2}) ⊂ [0, ρ], so this is
≤ ε. With G = 1 + Q in the whitened coordinates, the critical value is

    v = b†Gb − b†GZ (Z†GZ)^{-1} Z†Gb = |b|² + b†Qb − (b†QZ)(1 + Z†QZ)^{-1}(Z†Qb),

since Z†b = 0. The inverse exists with norm ≤ 1/(1−ε), so
|v − |b|²| ≤ ε|b|² + ε²|b|²/(1−ε) = ε|b|²/(1−ε). The right-hand side is a rational
function of s, equal to the real minimum for real s, so it is the analytic
continuation. The last inequality is Theorem 6c.3(1). ∎

The point is that the whole composite of j steps is **one** Schur complement, so no
constant is lost per step: the estimate does not go through Lemma G's induction and
needs no contraction θ' < 1. This is the quadratic-sector analogue of Lemma 6b.1:
for |s − σ| ≤ θ/ρ, θ < 1, the relative distance is ≤ θ/(1−θ), uniformly in N, j.
`t76_complex_disc.py` (`.out`) checks the bound for L = 2 (N = 2, 3) and L = 3
(N = 2) at ε between 0.22 and 0.90: the largest ratio of the numerical radius of the
whitened difference to ε/(1−ε) is 0.775, attained at j = 0, where the ratio is
exactly 1 − ε. (The script's zero-mode divide warnings, at K = 0 only and masked,
are filtered from the `.out`.)

In the abelian Gaussian skeleton, for all s ∈ [0,1],

#### 6c.6 What remains of (I-S), and where convexity does not suffice

Closed here (quadratic sector, abelian, flat): C1 coercivity and two-sided bound
along the real orbit, uniform in N, j (Theorem 6c.3(1)); C2 tangent domination for
real s (6c.3(2)-(3)); C3 an N- and j-independent complex disc (6c.4).

Not closed. Each item is a step where an *order* statement (Loewner sandwich,
concavity) does not imply what is needed:

- **R1. Generated terms (the published (A_s)/(H2) input, now at re-centred data).**
  The true step is Φ_j = Σ + G_j, with G_j the fluctuation corrections (T2, T3 of
  §6b.1: one-loop determinant, polymer terms; and the cubic and higher orders). G_j is
  not monotone or concave in the interpolated data, so convexity says nothing about
  its s-dependence. Balaban's bounds (note 42 Theorem 3, given (A_s)) are for the
  fluctuation measure of the classical action plus *small* irrelevant terms. Here the
  Gaussian part must be re-centred on Π_j^{(s)}, not S. What Theorem 6c.3 supplies is
  the hypothesis such a re-centring needs: S ≤ Π_j^{(s)} ≤ W_{j+1} with constants
  independent of s, N. That Balaban's estimates are stable under this replacement is
  the **remaining analytic part of (I-S)**. It is the (A_s)/(H2) input, not a new one,
  but its formulation at Π^{(s)} instead of Π^{(0)} is new and not proved.
- **R2. Kernel locality of Π_j^{(s)} for j ≥ 1 and s ∈ (0,1).** At level N (j = 0) the
  kernel is affine in s, so decay rates and the moments M_2(q), ‖v‖_∞ of note 51 Lemma 4
  are bounded by the max over the endpoints. For j ≥ 1, Π_j^{(s)} is a Schur
  complement, a nonlinear function of s; a Loewner sandwich does not bound off-diagonal
  kernel entries or the cross terms between smooth fibre elements and rough ones. Such
  cross terms control the minimizer's smoothness: with S ≤ Π ≤ CS on a fibre b + Z
  (b ⊥ Z), the Π-minimizer is b + z with z = −(Π_ZZ)^{-1}Π_Z b, and Π(b, z_r) =
  (Π−S)(b, z_r) can be as large as (C−1)|b||z_r| for a rough z_r, which the sandwich
  allows. So gradient bounds ‖∇B_s‖ ≲ M^{-1}‖B_s‖ (note 44 Theorem 1, proved for
  T_S) are not implied. Likely route: Combes-Thomas for the fibre Green function from
  the gap c = 1 and the exponential locality of Δ (T1, CMP 102b (190)). **Not carried
  out.** R2 is needed for (H1) at non-flat backgrounds (note 63 (iii)) and for the
  Lipschitz inequality (i) at interior s. *(T78, §6d: conditional reduction proved, via
  Combes-Thomas on potentials; open inputs (P), (L), (H) named there.)*
- **R3. Contraction θ' < 1 for complex perturbations in the full data.** The quadratic
  sector avoids it (6c.3), because the j-step composite is one Schur complement. For
  the coupled data (quadratic ⊕ generated ⊕ higher order) the induction of Lemma 6b.1
  still needs (i) with θ' < 1 on N_r. Convexity gives, for real perturbations, only
  non-expansion in the Thompson (log-ratio) metric, which is contraction factor 1; for
  complex ones it gives the factor 1/(1−ε) at each application if iterated. The gain
  C/L from irrelevance must come from R2.
- **R4. Non-abelian, non-flat.** Lemma 6c.2 and the Schur structure need the
  linearized average at a flat background (note 54). Away from flat backgrounds the
  step is the Schur complement plus O(ω+ε_0) corrections (note 63 Prop. 2); by Lemma 1
  there these are small perturbations of the s-form, but only once R2 gives the
  locality needed for (iii).
- **R5. Large fields (I-LF).** Untouched. For s < 0 in the Gaussian model positivity
  of S + sΔ fails at s = −1/ρ (the zero-free radius of §6b.5); the nonlinear lower
  bound I-LF(b) is T74.

**Verdict.** (I-S) along the real orbit holds in the quadratic sector by convexity and
coercivity, with c = 1 and the upper constant of the Wilson orbit, uniformly in
s, N, j, and gives the tangent bound at every s from s = 0. The part of (I-S) that
remains is R1 (generated terms at the re-centred Gaussian Π^{(s)}) and R2 (kernel
locality of the Schur complements at j ≥ 1), with R3-R5 downstream. Next concrete
step: Combes-Thomas for the composite fibre problem (6c.1) in the abelian model (R2),
which would give uniform exponential decay of Π_j^{(s)} and the gradient bound for
B_s, and then the one-step estimate of note 42 Theorem 3 with Π_j^{(s)} as Gaussian
part (R1).

### 6d. Kernel locality of the interpolated forms (T78)

**Question (R2 of 6c.6).** Is the kernel of Π_j^{(s)}, and of the minimizer map
Φ ↦ B_s(Φ) of (6c.1), exponentially decaying,
|kernel(x,y)| ≤ C e^{−μ_0|x−y|} in units of the level-(N−j) lattice, with C, μ_0
independent of s ∈ [0,1], j, N?

**Answer (conditional reduction, plus a numerical confirmation).** Theorem 6d.1
below proves the decay, with μ_0 and C explicit and independent of s, j, N, **given
three local-geometry inputs (P), (L), (H)** that are stated precisely in 6d.2 and are
**not proved here**. The proof is a Combes-Thomas (Agmon) argument for the composite
fibre problem (6c.1). It handles the obstruction expected for this route, that the fibre
Z = ker w ∩ ran d is not invariant under multiplication by e^{μ·x}, by cutting off
*potentials* instead of fluxes. Status:

- **conditional reduction (proved here):** Theorem 6d.1. For the Wilson orbit
  (s = 0; and s = 1, which is the Wilson orbit at depth j+1) it needs only (P) and
  (L), which are properties of the lattice and of w, not of the model. For
  0 < s < 1 it needs (H) in addition.
- **computation:** in the abelian Fourier model the potential-form kernel decays at
  rate 1.25-1.67 per coarse l¹-unit for L = 2, 3, j = 1, 2, 3, s = 0, ½, 1, and the
  rate converges as j grows (6d.4). The zero-extended *flux* form does **not** decay
  exponentially (it has the |x|^{-3} tail of the Wilson projector P), which is why the
  statement has to be made for pairings of closed fluxes (6d.1).
- **open:** (P), (L), (H) (6d.5).

#### 6d.1 Conventions, and what "kernel" means for a form on ran d

*Normalization.* (6c.1) as printed omits a factor. With the dimensionless units of
6c.1 (the fine Wilson form is L|B|² in coarse units), the j-step Schur complement is

    Π_j^{(s)}(Φ) = L^j · min { H(B) : B ∈ ran d_N, wB = Φ },   H = S + sΔ,   (6d.1)

where w = w^{(M)}, M = L^j, is the composite kernel of note 18 §8(a). A positive
constant is irrelevant for decay, but the coercivity c = 1 of Theorem 6c.3 refers to
this normalization (it is the one in `t76_convexity.py`, G = L^{-4}Σ...).
`t78_realspace_check.py` (`.out`) verifies (6d.1) by brute force: on the torus
(Z/8)³, L = 2, j = 1, the constrained minimum of (1−s)S + sΣ(S) over closed fluxes
with wB = Φ, times L, equals the Fourier symbol Π_1^{(s)}(K) of the aliasing formula
for s = 0, ½, 1 (spectra at all K ≠ 0 agree to 2e-14; the constraint residual is
1e-14).

*Units.* Work on the level-N lattice Λ = Z³ (fine units), M = L^j. The coarse lattice
is MZ³. A weight e^{ν·x} with ν = μ/M per fine unit is e^{μ·Y} per coarse unit. So
the decay rates below are **per level-(N−j) lattice unit**, as note 51 needs.

*Fluxes.* 𝔇 := ker(div) ⊂ ℓ²(Λ; R³), the closed fluxes (Bianchi: Σ_a ∂_a B_a = 0,
a *local* condition); on the torus 𝔇 = ran d in the sector of zero global flux.
w maps 𝔇 into the closed coarse fluxes 𝔇_c, because div_c w = w_0 div (the kernel
identity of note 18 §8(a)). For Φ ∈ 𝔇_c the fibre is F_Φ = {B ∈ 𝔇 : wB = Φ} = b + Z,
Z := 𝔇 ∩ ker w. Since H ≥ c_H > 0 (hypothesis (H)), the minimizer B(Φ) exists and is
unique (Lax-Milgram on the closed affine space F_Φ), and it is characterized by

    ⟨H B(Φ), z⟩ = 0   for all z ∈ Z.   (EL)

*Three notions of kernel.* Π is a form on 𝔇_c only, so "the kernel" is not
canonical:

- (K1) *off-diagonal decay on closed fluxes:* |Π(Φ, Φ')| ≤ C e^{−μ D}‖Φ‖‖Φ'‖ for
  closed Φ, Φ' with supports at coarse distance D. This is representation-free and is
  what Theorem 6d.1 proves.
- (K2) *the potential-form kernel* κ(ℓ,ℓ') := Π(d_cδ_ℓ, d_cδ_ℓ'), the kernel of
  a ↦ Π(d_c a) on coarse links; it is the special case Φ = d_cδ_ℓ of (K1) (a closed
  flux on 4 plaquettes), and it is what the numerics of 6d.4 measure.
- (K3) *a flux-operator representative:* a bounded symmetric π on ℓ²(plaquettes) with
  Π(Φ) = ⟨Φ, πΦ⟩ for closed Φ, with a decaying kernel. The natural representative,
  extension by zero off the transverse space, contains the projector P(K) and is
  **not** local: `t78_flux_tail.out` gives local power-law exponents 3.17, 3.19, 3.11
  between |x| = 8 and 16 for Π(W_1), Π(W_2) and for P itself (3.06, 3.06, 3.05 between
  16 and 24, where torus images start to contribute). A
  local representative, when it exists, is a different object. Note 51 Lemma 4 uses
  (K3) for polymer activities supported in a bounded X, where it follows from the
  Poincaré lemma on X. For Π_j^{(s)} it is the content of hypothesis (H) below, not
  something to be assumed silently.

*Facts about w (proved).* w_a(x) = b_M(x_a)∏_{i≠a}(b_M*s_M)(x_i), with b_M = M^{-1}1_{[0,M)}

#### 6d.3 Theorem 6d.1 (Combes-Thomas for the composite fibre; conditional reduction)

Put C_H := 1 + m_0 (this bounds ‖e^{φ}He^{−φ}‖ for every ν-Lipschitz weight with
ν ≤ ν_Δ, and H itself) and, for μ > 0,

    ε_1(μ) := 4 C_P μ e^{(3+r_P)μ},
    ε_2(μ) := C_L e^{r_Lμ} [ 4√3 μ e^{6√3μ} + e^{2√3μ} ε_1(μ) ],
    ε_3 := ε_1 + ε_2.

Call μ *admissible* if μ ≤ μ_Δ/2 and m_1 μ + C_H ε_3(μ) ≤ c_H/2.

**Theorem 6d.1.** Assume (P), (L), (H) with constants independent of M = L^j. Let
μ be admissible, K_u := (2C_H/c_H)(1+ε_3)e^{r_Lμ}, and let Φ, Φ' ∈ 𝔇_c have
finite supports S, S' ⊂ MZ³ (coarse plaquette positions, in fine coordinates) at
coarse distance D := dist(S,S')/M. Then for every s ∈ [0,1], j ≥ 0, N ≥ j:

1. (*decay of the minimizer map*) the fibre correction z' := B(Φ') − b(Φ')
   satisfies ‖z' 1_{dist(·,S') ≥ ℓ}‖ ≤ K_u e^{−μℓ/M} ‖b(Φ')‖ for all ℓ ≥ 0, and
   b(Φ') is supported within r_L M of S'. In coarse units the decay rate is μ.
2. (*decay of the form*) |Π_j^{(s)}(Φ,Φ')| ≤ C e^{−μD} ‖Φ‖ ‖Φ'‖ with
   C := C_L² C_H e^{r_Lμ}(e^{r_Lμ} + K_u).

*Explicit μ_0.* For μ ≤ 1, ε_3(μ) ≤ κ_3 μ with
κ_3 := 4C_Pe^{3+r_P} + C_L e^{r_L}[4√3e^{6√3} + 4C_Pe^{2√3+3+r_P}], so
μ_0 := min{1, μ_Δ/2, c_H/(2(m_1 + C_Hκ_3))} is admissible. For s = 0 and s = 1
(H = 1: c_H = C_H = 1, m_0 = m_1 = 0) μ_0 = min{1, 1/(2κ_3)} depends only on the
lattice constants C_P, r_P, C_L, r_L, not on L, j, N, s. For 0 < s < 1 the defect
enters only through (c_H, C_H, m_1, μ_Δ), constants of the single one-step object
Δ̂_L, and only as m_1 μ/M, so it matters less as j grows. (The constants are not
optimized.)

*Proof.* Fix φ_T(x) := ν min(dist(x,S'), T) with ν := μ/M ≤ μ, so φ_T is
ν-Lipschitz in the Euclidean metric; write φ = φ_T, E := e^{2φ}, and u := e^{φ}z'
(in ℓ² because φ is bounded; the constants below do not depend on T, and T → ∞ at the
end by monotone convergence). Let B' := B(Φ') = b' + z', b' := b(Φ'), z' ∈ Z.

*Step 1: a test fibre element built from a potential.* By (P), A' := Λ_0 z' satisfies
dA' = z'. Put z_0 := d(E A') ∈ 𝔇 and

    z := z_0 − b(w z_0).

This is in Z: z_0 is closed, w z_0 is a closed coarse flux (w maps 𝔇 into 𝔇_c), so
b(wz_0) is defined, closed, and w z = w z_0 − w z_0 = 0. (This is where the
non-invariance of Z under multiplication by e^{2φ} is handled: E z' is not closed,
but d(E A') is, and it differs from E z' by a commutator that carries one derivative
of E acting on A', which is of size ν|A'| ≲ (μ/M)·M|z'| = μ|z'| by (P).)

*Step 2: z ≈ E z'.* Write z_0 = E z' + δ with δ = (dE − Ed)A'. At a site x the
curl components of δ are sums of two terms (E(x+e_k) − E(x))A_l(x+e_k), and
|E(x+e_k) − E(x)| ≤ (e^{2ν}−1)E(x). Hence
‖e^{−φ}δ‖ ≤ 2(e^{2ν}−1)e^{ν}‖e^{φ}A'‖. By (P), ‖e^{φ}A'‖ ≤ e^{r_Pμ} C_P M ‖u‖
(e^{φ(x)} ≤ e^{φ(y)}e^{νr_PM} on the range of Λ_0), and M(e^{2ν}−1) ≤ 2μe^{2ν}, so

    ‖e^{−φ}δ‖ ≤ ε_1(μ) ‖u‖.

Next g := w z_0 = w(E z') + wδ. Since w z' = 0, for a coarse point Y
(wEz')(Y) = Σ_x w(x−Y)(E(x) − E(Y))z'(x), and
|E(x)−E(Y)| ≤ 2ν|x−Y| e^{2φ(Y)+2ν|x−Y|}, e^{φ(Y)} ≤ e^{φ(x)+ν|x−Y|}. With
|x−Y| ≤ R_w ≤ 2√3M and (W1),
‖e^{−φ}wEz'‖ ≤ 4√3 μ e^{6√3μ} M^{1/2}‖u‖, and likewise
‖e^{−φ}wδ‖ ≤ e^{2√3μ} M^{1/2}‖e^{−φ}δ‖. By (L) (weights move by e^{r_Lμ}),

    ‖e^{−φ}b(g)‖ ≤ ε_2(μ) ‖u‖,   so   ‖e^{−φ}(z − E z')‖ ≤ ε_3(μ) ‖u‖.

*Step 3: the Euler-Lagrange equation.* (EL) with this z gives
0 = ⟨Hz', Ez'⟩ + ⟨Hb', Ez'⟩ + ⟨HB', z − Ez'⟩ =: T_1 + T_2 + T_3.
T_1 = ⟨e^{φ}He^{−φ}u, u⟩ = ⟨Hu,u⟩ + s⟨(e^{φ}Δ̂e^{−φ} − Δ̂)u,u⟩ ≥ (c_H − m_1ν)‖u‖²,
by (H)(i) and the Schur test |e^{ν|x−y|}−1| ≤ ν|x−y|e^{ν|x−y|} (ν ≤ ν_Δ). On

and μ is admissible,

    (c_H/2)‖u‖² ≤ C_H e^{r_Lμ}(1+ε_3)‖b'‖‖u‖,   i.e.   ‖e^{φ}z'‖ ≤ K_u ‖b'‖.

Since e^{φ_T} ≥ e^{ν min(ℓ,T)} on {dist ≥ ℓ}, letting T → ∞ gives item 1.

*Step 4: the form.* With (EL) for B' tested on z ∈ Z,
Π(Φ,Φ')/M = ⟨B(Φ), H B'⟩ = ⟨b, Hb'⟩ + ⟨Hb, z'⟩ (⟨z,HB'⟩ = 0 by (EL) for B'
tested on z = z(Φ) ∈ Z). First term: b and b' are supported within r_LM of S
and S'. If D > 2r_L they are at fine distance ≥ M(D − 2r_L) and, since H = 1 + sΔ̂,

*Remarks.* (1) *Why potentials.* Cutting off a closed flux gives a field with
divergence ∇χ·B, and the local correction (a Bogovskii/divergence solver on the
transition region of width ℓ_χ) has norm ≈ C‖B‖, not small: the constant does not
improve with ℓ_χ. Cutting off the potential gives an error of relative size μ because
the potential of a fibre element is bounded by M times the element (the Poincaré
scaling, input (P)). (2) *Where M cancels:* ν = μ/M, |A| ≲ M|z|, ‖w‖ ≲ M^{1/2},
‖b‖ ≲ M^{-1/2}. All errors are O(μ) with M-independent constants, which is why one
composite Schur complement gives μ_0 independent of j with no per-step loss; iterating
the one-step estimate j times would lose a constant per step. (3) *What is trivial:*
in the flux picture the fibre form is coercive with constant c_H (= 1 for the orbit)
and no Poincaré inequality enters Steps 3-4; the Poincaré content of the fibre is
entirely in (P). (4) A pseudo-inverse/Riesz-projector Combes-Thomas on the fibre in
the potential picture, with the spectral gap ∼M^{-2} of d^*Hd on ker Q, only gives
a rate ∼M^{-3} per fine unit, i.e. M^{-2} per coarse unit: the gauge directions
make that route non-uniform. (5) On a torus use the periodic distance; the
construction and constants are unchanged. (6) The statement is for pairings of
closed fluxes (K1), hence includes (K2): κ(ℓ,ℓ') = Π(d_cδ_ℓ, d_cδ_ℓ'),
‖d_cδ_ℓ‖ = 2, gives |κ(ℓ,ℓ')| ≤ 4C e^{2μ_0} e^{−μ_0|ℓ−ℓ'|} (the supports are within 1 of the links).

#### 6e.1 Which reading of (I-LF)(b) the σ = −τ step uses

Note 67 §5 distinguishes two readings. (1) V|_Z = V on Z, flat elsewhere, and the
claim is D_N(V|_Z) − c_N ≥ −C_-β_NA_Z(V|_Z) − e_ent|Z| log β_N. (2) The weight of the
actual configuration V, with whatever small-field exterior it has, compared with the
vacuum value; this needs the relative form (R).

The step in question writes |e^{sD_N}ρ_0|(V) = ρ_0(V) e^{−τD_N(V)} at σ = −τ and
bounds e^{−τD_N(V)} by exp(τ[C_-β_NA_Z + e_ent|Z| log β_N]). The left side is the
weight of V itself, with its exterior present. It is not the weight of V|_Z. So the
step needs a lower bound for the part of D_N(V) that is attributable to Z, with V
arbitrary outside Z. That is reading 2.

*Reading 1 cannot suffice.* In the abelian Gaussian model (note 67 §0, Prop. 67.1)
D_N(V) = β_NQ(V) + c_N′ with Q a quadratic form. Write a := V|_Z = V − V^Z (the
bond variables on Z; V^Z = V with Z set to 0, additive in the model) and b := V^Z.
Then, exactly,

    D_N(V) − D_N(V^Z) = β_N [ Q(a) + 2 Q(a, b) ],        D_N(V|_Z) − c_N′ = β_N Q(a).

Reading 1 controls the first term (Q(a) ≥ −(L²−1)A_N(a), Theorem 64.1; note 67
§3.3(a)). The weight of V contains the cross term 2β_NQ(a, b), and (R) fails
because of it (Prop. 67.3(3), §3.4). (I-LF)(b′) is exactly a bound for Q(a) + 2Q(a, b).
The σ ∈ [0,1] and σ > 1 sides of Lemma 6b.2 use only (I-LF)(a), an upper bound,
and are unchanged. The task therefore does not reduce; the σ = −τ side is redone
below.

*One more repair.* The displayed σ = −τ bound of Lemma 6b.2 writes
exp(−β_NA_P(1 − τC_-) + …), i.e. it identifies the near energy A_Z of (I-LF)(b)
(note 67: the plaquettes touching Z) with the energy A_P of the large-field plaquettes.
They differ: A_Z ≥ A_P. §6e.3 keeps both.

#### 6e.2 Conventions and hypotheses

(C1) *Plaquette energies.* A_x(V) ≥ 0 depends on the four bond variables of x;
A_S := Σ_{x∈S} A_x. For a set Z of coarse bonds, N(Z) := plaquettes containing a bond
of Z, A_Z(V) := A_{N(Z)}(V) (as in note 67), and V^Z := V with the bond variables on Z
set to 1. For x ∉ N(Z) (written x ∌ Z in note 67) A_x(V^Z) = A_x(V).

(C2) *Large fields.* p = p(g_k) is the small-field threshold of the scale considered;
x is small-field in V iff β_NA_x(V) ≤ p²/2 (note 67 §4). P = P(V) is the set of
large-field plaquettes, so β_NA_P(V) ≥ (p²/2)|P|. For a plaquette set P let Z(P) be

and A_{Z(P)} ≥ A_P. In particular **every plaquette x ∉ N(Z(P(V))) is small-field.**

#### 6e.3 The weight bounds on the strip (Proposition 6e.1)

−τ[D_N(V) − D_N(V^Z)] ≤ τ[ C_-β_NA_Z + 𝒯 + e_ent|Z| log β_N ], with
𝒯 := C_Tβ_N Σ_{c∈Z} Σ_{x∉N(Z)} e^{−m d(x,c)} A_x(V). By (C2) every x ∉ N(Z) is
small-field, β_NA_x ≤ p²/2, and for each c ∈ Z the set {x ∉ N(Z)} lies in
{x ∉ N({c})}. So

    𝒯 ≤ C_T |Z| N_m p²/2 ≤ c_Z C_T N_m (p²|P|/2) ≤ c_Z C_T N_m β_NA_P.

Hence |F_s| ≤ exp( −β_NA_Z(1 − τC_-) + τ c_Z C_T N_m β_NA_P + τ e_ent|Z| log β_N ).
Because τC_- ≤ ½ and A_Z ≥ A_P, the first term is at most −β_NA_P(1 − τC_-), so

    |F_s| ≤ exp( −β_NA_P [1 − τ(C_- + c_Z C_T N_m)] + τ c_Z e_ent |P| log β_N )
          ≤ exp( −½ β_NA_P + t_0 c_Z e_ent |P| log β_N ),

since τ(C_- + c_ZC_TN_m) ≤ ½. As ½ ≥ c_1/2 this is the claimed bound.

(iii) The exponent −(c_1/2)β_NA_P + t_0c_Ze_ent|P| log β_N is a sum over the
plaquettes of P (A_P = Σ_i A_{P_i}, |P| = Σ|P_i|), hence the product form for any
partition. Last, β_NA_{P_i} ≥ (p²/2)|P_i| gives (c_1/2)β_NA_{P_i} ≥ (c_1/4)p²|P_i|, and
(LF-E) bounds the entropy by (c_1/8)p²|P_i|. ∎

*Size of the tail.* The tail 𝒯 is not small in general:
½C_TN_m = 76 p² per bond (L = 2, m = 2), 4 bonds per plaquette of P at most, with
p²|P| ≤ 2β_NA_P. So c_ZC_TN_m ≈ 6·10² (L = 2) and ≈ 1.4·10³ (L = 3, C_T = 20.6).
These are crude: every exterior plaquette is taken to sit at the small-field
threshold at once. The sharp constant of note 67's linear programme (4.7 p² per
bond, L = 2) applies only to V flat at Z. Numbers (model, m = 2): L = 2,
(C_-, C_T) = (3, 8.8): s_0^{LF′} = 1/(2·615.5) = 8.1·10⁻⁴. L = 3, (8, 20.6):
3.5·10⁻⁴. They assume (LF-b′) holds with these constants for all Z. The entry
c_1/(2(1−c_1)) is the larger one for every c_1 ≥ 2·10⁻³, so the unknown SU(2) value
of c_1 does not affect these numbers. (In the model the global form of (LF-a) holds
with c_1 = 1, because Q ≤ 0, i.e. Lβ_NA_min ≥ β_NA_N (proved by Lemma 6c.2 above, note 67 §3.3(a)); the localized form is not
checked.)

#### 6e.4 Large fields at distance: merging, factorization, and the per-polymer route

**(a) The union form needs no merging.** In Proposition 6e.1 (LF-b′) is applied to
the union Z of all large-field regions of V. A plaquette x ∉ N(Z) is small-field,
so the tail never sees another large-field region: what note 67 §5 called the
charge C_Te^{−mr}β_NA_{Z′} to Z from a second region Z′ at distance r is, in the
union form, part of C_-β_NA_Z with the *same* uniform C_-. The weight bound is then
a product over any partition of P, in particular over the r-clusters of P for any
radius r ≥ 2. The radius r_* = 2 is the least one for which the near sets N(Z(P_i))
of distinct clusters are disjoint (x ∈ N(Z(y)) implies d(x,y) ≤ 1), so that
A_Z = Σ_i A_{Z_i}; the bound itself only uses A_Z ≥ A_P and does not need it.

**(b) The per-polymer route and the value of r_0.** Suppose one wants to apply
(b′) to each cluster separately, with the others in the exterior. Take clusters
P_1, …, P_n of P(V) with disjoint near sets (radius r_* = 2) and the individual
factors F_s(Z_i;V), Z_i = Z(P_i). For x ∈ N(Z_j), j ≠ i, A_x(V) is not small; the
charge from the tail of Z_i is τC_Tβ_N Σ_{c∈Z_i} e^{−m d(x,c)} A_x(V). Summing over
i ≠ j, Σ_{c∈∪_{i≠j}Z_i} e^{−m d(x,c)} ≤ Ñ_m for every x, so the total charge to Z_j is
at most τ C_T Ñ_m β_NA_{Z_j}. The same chain as in (ii) gives, if
τ(C_- + c_ZC_TN_m + C_TÑ_m) ≤ ½,

    ∏_i |F_s(Z_i;V)| ≤ exp( −½ β_NA_P + τ c_Z e_ent |P| log β_N ).

There is **no condition on the distance** between regions. In terms of note 67's
formula, the condition τ ≤ 1/(2c_ZC_TN_m) already gives 2τC_T/c_1 ≤ 1/(c_1c_ZN_m), so

    r_0 = m⁻¹ log(2τC_T/c_1) ≤ −m⁻¹ log(c_1 c_Z N_m) < 0   whenever c_1 > 1/(4N_m)

(model: c_1 = 1, m = 2, τ = s_0^{LF′}: r_0 = ½ log(2·8.1·10⁻⁴·8.8) = −2.1). The
merging radius is therefore max(2, r_0) = 2 in every case with c_1 > 1/(4N_m).
A distance-dependent refinement (charges decaying like T_m(r) := sup_x
Σ_{c: d(x,c)≥r} e^{−m d(x,c)}; T_2(2) = 5.55, T_2(3) = 1.50, T_2(4) = 0.34,
T_2(5) = 0.07) only improves constants.

**(c) Failed attempt: assembling the weight from the individual factors.** The
product of individual factors is not the weight. In the Gaussian model, for Z = Z_1 ⊔ Z_2 and a_i = V|_{Z_i},

    [D_N(V) − D_N(V^Z)] = [D_N(V) − D_N(V^{Z_1})] + [D_N(V) − D_N(V^{Z_2})] − 2β_N Q(a_1, a_2),

and Q(a_1,a_2) is bilinear in the bond variables a_i = V|_{Z_i}. Those are
gauge-dependent: a gauge transformation at the head vertex of c_1 changes V_{c_1}
by an arbitrary amount at fixed plaquette fluxes. Each first difference is bounded
below uniformly in the gauge (that is the content of (b′), through the minimization
over the free bond variable, Prop. 67.5), but the cross term is not bounded by the
near energies. So the lower bound for the union cannot be assembled from the lower
bounds for its components. The sequential route (flatten Z_1, then apply (b′) to
(V^{Z_1}, Z_2)) fails at one exact step: the tail of Z_2 then contains
A_x(V^{Z_1}) for x ∈ N(Z_1), the energy of plaquettes with some bond set to 1, which
is not controlled by the fluxes of V (it depends on V_{c_1}). In SU(2), A_x ≤ 2, and
bounding A_x(V^{Z_1}) by 2 charges Z_2 with about 2τC_Tβ_N e^{−mr} per plaquette of
N(Z_1), to be absorbed in p²; that would force r of order m⁻¹ log(β_N/p²) (an
estimate, not carried out). The Gaussian model has no such bound. That route is
not pursued. **What closes the lemma is the union form of (LF-b′).**
Its uniformity over disconnected Z with the single-component constants is thus the
substantive extra assumption of §6e.

#### 6e.5 The KP summation with the larger connectivity radius (Proposition 6e.2)

**Proposition 6e.2 (proved here).** Let r ≥ 2, Δ_r := 3(2r+1)³, c_d(r) := log(eΔ_r).

(i) *Counting.* If κ ≥ 4c_d(r), then for every block □
    Σ_{Z∋□, r-connected} e^{−κ d(Z)} ≤ 2.
(ii) *Large-field factor against entropy.* Let w(Z) := exp(−(c_1/8)p²|P(Z)|) be the
large-field factor of Proposition 6e.1 for the polymer with large-field set P(Z), and
p_*² := inf_k p²(g_k) (the infimum is at k = 0 and tends to ∞ as ε_0 → 0). If

    p_*² ≥ (8/c_1) ( ½ κ r + log(4eΔ_r) ),

then Σ_{Z∋□} w(Z) e^{½κ d(Z)} ≤ ⅓. This gives the KP condition of note 49 §6,
Σ_{X∋□} |activity(X)| e^{a(X)} ≤ a(□), with a(X) = ½κd(X) + 1, for the
large-field polymers with activity prefactor ≤ 1: the left side is at most
e/3 < 1 = a(□), since d(□) = 0 for a single block; and for a polymer of |X|
blocks the incompatible sum is at most |X| e/3 ≤ ½κ(|X| − 1) + 1 ≤ a(X)
(d(X) ≥ |X| − 1, κ ≥ 4). [Audit 2026-10-09, T82: the earlier wording
"≤ ½ ≤ ½κ" with a(X) = ½κd(X) needed d(□) = 1, but d(□) = 0 here.]

*Proof.* (i) The number of connected subsets of j vertices containing a fixed vertex
in a graph of maximal degree Δ is at most (eΔ)^{j−1} (standard). Here the graph is
the r-link graph, of degree < Δ_r, and d(Z) ≥ j − 1 (each tree link has length ≥ 1).
So the sum is at most Σ_{j≥1} (eΔ_r e^{−κ})^{j−1} ≤ 2, because eΔ_re^{−κ} ≤ e^{−3c_d} ≤ ½.
(ii) Polymers with |P| = j plaquettes of the large-field set containing a given
plaquette number at most (eΔ_r)^j, and d(Z) ≤ r(j−1) ≤ rj (a tree of links of
length ≤ r). So the sum is at most Σ_{j≥1} (eΔ_r e^{½κr − c_1p²/8})^j. The hypothesis
makes the ratio at most ¼, so the sum is at most ⅓ ≤ ½. ∎

So the extra entropy of the radius is the constant log Δ_r = log 3 + 3 log(2r+1),
plus κ r from the tree length. Both are independent of ε_0 and of N, and the
large-field exponent c_1p_*²/8 grows without bound as ε_0 → 0. **KP closes for
every fixed r_*.** Numbers: r_* = 2, Δ_2 = 375, c_d(2) = 6.93, κ ≥ 27.7, and
c_1p_*² ≥ 8(27.7 + 8.3) = 288. The constants C_T, N_m, τ enter only through
s_0^{LF′} and r_0, not through this condition.

The requirement κ ≥ 4c_d(r_*) in (LF-act) is not an extra constraint on the
large-field polymers. Since d(Z) ≤ r_*|P|, the weight bound itself gives
e^{−(c_1/8)p²|P|} ≤ e^{−κ_LF d(Z)} with κ_LF := c_1p_*²/(8r_*), and κ_LF ≥ 4c_d(r_*)
as soon as p_*² ≥ 32 r_* c_d(r_*)/c_1 (r_* = 2: 443/c_1). So the large-field factor
supplies the decay rate that the larger connectivity radius requires.

#### 6e.6 Lemma 6b.2′

**Lemma 6b.2′ ((H4b) under (I-LF)(b′); proved here from (LF-a), (LF-b′), Lemma 6b.1,
(LF-E) and (LF-act)).** Assume the hypotheses of Lemma 6b.2 with (I-LF)(b) replaced by
(LF-b′), and let

    s_0^{LF′} := min{ c_1/(2(1−c_1)) [∞ if c_1 = 1],  1/(2(C_- + 4C_TN_m)) },
    s_1′ := min(s_0, s_0^{LF′}),

with s_0 from Lemma 6b.1. Take any connectivity radius r_* ≥ max{2, r_0}, where
r_0 := m⁻¹ log(2 s_0^{LF′} C_T/c_1) is the merging radius of note 67 §5 (r_0 < 0
whenever c_1 > 1/(4N_m)), and assume (LF-E) with t_0 = s_1′ and (LF-act) at radius r_*
(so κ ≥ 4c_d(r_*)). Then for every real s ∈ [0,1] and every scale k = N − j,

    Σ_{Z meeting the blocks of scale k} |∂_s 𝒜_k^{(s)}(Z)|  ≤  54 n_0³ ε_0³ C_LF ε_k^{q−3} / s_1′ ,

with no dependence on N or the volume. For C_T = 0 the radius s_0^{LF′} is the old
s_0^{LF}.

*Proof.* (1) *Weights.* For s′ with dist(s′,[0,1]) ≤ s_1′, Proposition 6e.1 (with
t_0 = s_1′) bounds the region weight by ∏ exp(−(c_1/8)p²|P_i|) for every partition of
the large-field set, in particular for its r_*-clusters. This is the premise of
(LF-act). At s′ ∈ [0,1] it is Balaban's large-field factor of (H4a); on the strip it
has the same form with c_1 replaced by c_1/2 in the exponent of β_NA_P, and (LF-E)
is the lemma's condition p²(g_k) ≫ log β_N.
(2) *Activities.* By (LF-act), |𝒜_k^{(s′)}(Z)| ≤ C_LFε_k^qe^{−κd(Z)} on the open strip
dist(s′,[0,1]) < s_1′, and s′ ↦ 𝒜_k^{(s′)}(Z) is analytic there. (3) *Cauchy.* For real
s ∈ [0,1] the open disc |s′ − s| < s_1′ lies in the open strip, since
dist(s′,[0,1]) ≤ |s′ − s|. Cauchy on circles of radius ρ < s_1′ and ρ ↑ s_1′ give
|∂_s𝒜_k^{(s)}(Z)| ≤ C_LFε_k^qe^{−κd(Z)}/s_1′. (4) *Counting.* Polymers meeting a block
□ contain a block within distance 1 of □, 27 choices; by Proposition 6e.2(i) each
contributes at most 2. So Σ_{Z meeting □} |∂_s𝒜_k^{(s)}(Z)| ≤ 54 C_LFε_k^q/s_1′. (5)
*Blocks.* Summing over the (n_0L^k)³ = n_0³ε_0³ε_k^{−3} blocks of scale k, as in
Lemma 6b.2, gives the claim. Proposition 6e.2(ii) is the statement that the
large-field factor beats the entropy of the radius r_*, so that (LF-act) with
polymers r_*-connected is the same kind of hypothesis as at radius 1; it is not used
in the chain (1)-(5). ∎

q ≥ 4 makes the correction to θ′ in (H4) decay, as before.
